Skill read: /Users/travisseitler/.codex/skills/copywriter/SKILL.md. Read-only review of current source and r2 design contract/guidelines; historical evidence screenshots inspected for composition, but source governs current text. No app files changed. Assumed audience: people who read Scripture elsewhere and want a quiet, private record of passages and notes. No market or behavior evidence is asserted.

## Overall direction

The calm voice and verbs “Log a reading” / “Save reading” are strong. Preserve those. The main weakness is competing poetic lines around a relatively precise tool, coupled with technical explanatory language in areas where plain task guidance would help. Reduce persistent ornamental copy; add just enough contextual utility copy where a choice or numerical interpretation needs it. Never imply Bible text, sync, automatic backups, or spiritual progress.

### social-media-marketer

# Social media marketer persona — current information design audit

Working assumption: a first-time visitor may arrive from a recommendation, shared screenshot, or link with little knowledge of Jot & Tittle. This review addresses the app's ability to turn that attention into a useful first action. It proposes no campaign, social publishing, or platform claims.

Evidence: first-use desktop/mobile screenshots supplied by parent; current src/main.tsx, src/EmptyMapDemo.tsx, src/TextView.tsx, src/PatternsView.tsx, src/DetailSurface.tsx and design contract. Behavior claims below derive from source; screenshots are composition evidence, not a user study.

## Most consequential findings

1. **The phone's first screen does not clearly say this is Bible reading history.** The desktop at least has “YOUR SCRIPTURE, MAPPED,” though that is a sidebar label competing with multiple slogans. The phone shows product name, “Verse map,” “Your first reading,” “Log your first reading,” and generic reading guidance. A cold visitor should not need the sample or About dialog to determine the subject. Replace the welcome paragraph with: **“Record the Bible passages you’ve read. Each square represents a verse; color shows when and how often you’ve recorded it.”** If the map meaning is split into its own visible line, keep welcome copy **“Record a chapter, a few verses, or several Bible passages together.”** State that it tracks recorded passages, never that it detects reading automatically.

2. **The first-use heading and CTA repeat the same instruction at three levels.** Screenshot has “Your first reading,” “Log your first reading,” and the same CTA; the topbar adds “Log a reading.” This spends mobile vertical space without new information. Keep a descriptive page heading **“Your Bible reading, mapped”**, one short welcome paragraph, and the primary CTA **“Log your first reading.”** Remove the visible duplicate card heading if the page heading identifies the section; preserve accessible structure deliberately. The topbar CTA can stay as the global action once there is data, but should not outrank or duplicate the first-use card action visually.

3. **Too many catchphrases dilute the identifiable promise.** Desktop simultaneously shows “A map of your reading,” “YOUR SCRIPTURE, MAPPED,” “Every verse. A little more visible,” the sidebar paragraph, “Made for attention, not achievement,” demo's “A little reading, a growing map,” and footer “Every verse leaves a mark.” Choose one identity line, preferably **“Your Bible reading, mapped.”** Give working UI operational labels. Remove the sidebar mini-grid/slogan paragraph cluster and redundant footer catchphrase; retain a short nonjudgmental statement in About or near Patterns where metrics create the relevant concern. The specific existing statement “These marks describe where you’ve read. They don’t measure your faith, your effort, or your worth.” is truthful and should not become a repeated banner.

4. **The demo caption adds mood but withholds the important interpretation.** Replace “A little reading, a growing map” with **“Example: readings add color to the map”** and “Illustrative demo” with **“Illustration — not your reading history.”** Keep Pause/Play. This prevents a screenshot or animated arrival from implying real personal data while showing the product outcome. A gentler acceptable alternative is “How recorded readings color your map” with “Illustration.”

5. **Exploration choices need a clearer hierarchy.** “Explore sample data” means datasets to a new visitor; this is a visual app about reading. Change to **“Try a sample reading map.”** Keep the sample banner clearly visible: **“Sample readings — your journal is separate.”** Return action **“Return to my journal.”** “Explore my empty map” is a third optional path; demote it to a small tertiary link with direct wording **“Open my empty map.”** Do not make the sample look like saved history or alter the sample isolation semantics.

6. **Important guidance is hidden while peripheral slogans are visible.** Page subtitles contain map explanation but CSS hides them. Put the map's necessary concept next to the actual map as **“Each square is a verse. Color shows recorded reading recency and frequency.”** On first use explain this in the welcome paragraph; avoid showing it twice in that same state. On mobile, provide “How it works” as an available help entry rather than rely on desktop-only placement. Verify the actual current help visibility before implementation.

7. **“Text” mode risks implying bundled scripture.** Actual Text view lists references, counts, dates and notes; About says scripture text is not bundled. Use mode label **“Verse list”** and heading **“Verse references and reading history.”** Add concise local help **“References, counts, dates, and your notes. Bible text is not included.”** Preserve keyboard/assistive meaning and coordinate both labels. This guards against wrong expectations when someone arrives from a screenshot.

8. **Trust is stronger when literal.** Keep “Saved in this browser” where status matters and the current first-use backup sentence. Footer “Private. Local. Yours.” may stay as the single trust signature only if browser/device limitation remains visible. Prefer the factual **“Saved in this browser on this device.”** Preserve Your data's download confirmation limitation, excluded Trash, all-journal export scope, and storage clearing warning; these are necessary practical instructions, not slogan clutter. Do not imply cloud sync, accounts, or automatic backups.

9. **Patterns microcopy exposes internal counting vocabulary.** Replace “Frequencies deduplicate shared encounters; session totals count reading records. Map geography does not limit Patterns.” with **“Shared copies of the same reading count once in verse frequencies. Session totals count all reading records. Book and passage filters on the map do not limit these patterns.”** Single-journal version **“Verse frequencies count reading records in this journal. Book and passage filters on the map do not limit these patterns.”** Need domain verification that “shared copies” fully captures encounter semantics before adopting. Preserve the distinction rather than simplify it away. “Evidence view,” “geography,” and “frequency greater than one” are poor cold-arrival language; use **“current journal and date filters”** and **“Verses recorded more than once”** where those accurately match behavior.

## Amount of text by area

Less: first-use headings, decorative sidebar slogans, footer slogans, “THE BIG PICTURE”/similar eyebrows, repeated passage examples in placeholder plus helper.
More or better placed: Bible subject on first use; one-square/one-verse meaning near map; local storage limit before reliance; Verse list's no-Bible-text clarification; visible contextual map interaction hint (tap/select a square to see its reference and reading history, with valid keyboard instructions accessible).
Keep: live parser preview, advanced reference examples in disclosure, edit/delete/import consequences, sample banner, filtered-empty recovery, backup limitations, metric legend and count semantics.

On Verse list, can they tell references from Bible text? Review screenshots at 320px and desktop and verify the relevant labels do not wrap or disappear unexpectedly.

These are hypotheses about comprehension, not measured conversion results.
